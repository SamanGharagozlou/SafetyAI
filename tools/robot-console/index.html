Posture

The robot settles out of standing on its own when idle. Gestures and walks are refused unless it is standing — tick auto stand-up below and the console will do it for you.

Move

Speed is a fraction of the cap (0.6 m/s / 1.0 rad/s), so 1.0 means 0.6 m/s. Motion always auto-stops when the duration elapses.

Walk a measured distance

Closed-loop on odometry and accurate to a few cm, unlike the timed move above which undershoots. Blocks until the walk finishes — about 7 s for 30 cm.

Gait

Voice

No phone can send this call to a Bluetooth speaker. Confirmed on iPhone and Android, Safari and Chrome. It is a Bluetooth limitation, not a browser one — see below. Fastest fix: plug the speaker in with a cable (AUX or USB-C). Otherwise run the voice from the Mac and keep the phone for the robot controls.
Pick both before you press Talk. Names appear once the microphone has been allowed, and a speaker paired later shows up on its own. Phones list no output devices at all — iOS has no setSinkId, and Chrome on Android does not expose output selection either, because the OS owns the routing.

Bluetooth speaker plays music but not the call? The profile is the problem, not the phone. A2DP carries audio one way only — it is a playback profile and has no microphone channel, which is why music is flawless. A call needs two directions at once, so the phone switches to HFP, and most Bluetooth speakers implement no usable HFP. With no two-way route available the phone falls back to its own speaker and mic. Same on Android as on iOS, for the same reason.

What actually works. A cable — AUX or USB-C — sidesteps Bluetooth entirely and is the quickest fix. A Bluetooth headset has HFP and will route, at mono 8–16 kHz. Or run voice from a desktop OS: macOS, Windows and Linux route input and output independently, so an A2DP speaker plus any mic works, and the dropdown above becomes usable. Releasing the mic between turns would restore A2DP on a phone but costs barge-in, which is a worse trade for a greeter.
