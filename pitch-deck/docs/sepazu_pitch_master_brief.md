# Sepazu Pitch Deck Master Brief

## Complete slide content, spoken script, visual direction, assets, evidence rules, and handoff instructions

**Purpose:** This document is the single source of truth for creating a
new presentation in PowerPoint or another slide tool. It is written for
a presentation designer or another AI agent. Follow the slide order,
preserve the factual boundaries, and use the real media placeholders
listed below.

**Project:** Sepazu\
**Event:** Leukerbad Robotics Hackathon 2026\
**Track:** Autonomous Urban Services\
**Pitch format:** 4 minutes total, followed by 2 minutes of Q&A\
**Audience:** Hackathon judges, potential customers, partners, and
investors\
**Primary objective:** Win support for a paid pilot with the thermal spa
where the robot was deployed, gain introductions to other tourism
venues, and open investor conversations.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 1. Core pitch idea

## One sentence

**Sepazu is building a service robot for tourism and hospitality,
supported by an independent edge based safety monitoring and assurance
layer, so guests can get help even when staff are busy.**

## The story the presentation must tell

1.  Tourism venues welcome guests, but staff cannot always be available
    to answer every question or guide every visitor.
2.  The team focused on a real tourism setting: a thermal spa, where
    visitors need information, directions, and assistance while
    reception or service staff may be occupied.
3.  The robot creates a simple guest journey: a visitor notices it,
    scans a QR code, connects through a phone, asks questions, sees
    gestures, and can request an escort.
4.  A robot operating around people needs more than an engaging
    interface. It needs an independent layer that monitors its state and
    intent, predicts potentially unsafe next states, flags anomalies,
    and alerts an operator.
5.  The team deployed the robot in a real customer environment and
    observed guest engagement. Staff also suggested a possible outdoor
    welcome and sales use case.
6.  During the hackathon, the team integrated an additional Jetson
    computer, navigation, escort capability, audio and voice
    interaction, and gestures such as nodding.
7.  The next opportunity is to extend the same service into a local
    tourism guide near tourist information points, available when the
    information centre is closed.
8.  The business is a service subscription. The customer pays for access
    and operating time, not ownership of the robot hardware.
9.  The ask is a paid pilot, introductions to more tourism customers,
    and investor support to scale the service.

## Positioning

-   Lead with the **guest service problem**, not with the robot
    hardware.
-   Make the thermal spa deployment the concrete example that narrows
    the broad hospitality problem.
-   Make the safety layer the differentiator. Do not describe it as a
    certified safety system.
-   Present the customer feedback as qualitative validation. Do not
    invent adoption rates, interaction counts, purchase intent, or a
    customer commitment.
-   Present the 24 hour local guide as a future direction unless it is
    already deployed and proven.
-   Sell the service, not the robot hardware.

## Suggested product description

**A guest service robot with an independent edge based safety monitoring
and assurance layer.**

Use the name **Clawmetry** only for the team's monitoring dashboard. Do
not invent further product features or capabilities for Clawmetry.

shorten transitions and optional phrases first. Do not remove the safety

edit the video rather than silently extending the pitch.

## Slide 4. Safety monitoring and assurance layer

### Purpose

Explain the main differentiator: an independent edge computer monitors
robot behaviour and can alert an operator. Show why the behaviour around
people should differ from operation in a clear area.

### Exact on slide text

**THE ROBOT MUST ADAPT TO THE PEOPLE AROUND IT.**

Two comparison panels:

**CLEAR AREA**\
Full capabilities available

**PEOPLE NEARBY**\
Restricted control state

Core words:

**MONITOR. PREDICT. ALERT.**

Architecture strip:

`ROBOT STACK`\
`INDEPENDENT JETSON EDGE LAYER`\
`FLEET OPERATOR ALERT`

Small caption: `Independent monitoring alongside the robot’s main stack`

### Visual direction

-   Make this a clear side by side comparison.
-   Left panel: the robot operating alone in a clear area.
-   Right panel: the robot near children or guests, with a visibly more
    restricted behaviour.
-   Use the real photos. Do not create a synthetic child or robot scene.
-   Include the Clawmetry dashboard screenshot as a supporting image,
    not as an unreadable full slide.
-   Show the Jetson mounted on the robot if there is a suitable photo.
-   Use a lock icon or restrained visual indicator for the restricted
    state.
-   Reserve coral red for the actual alert or breach indicator only.
-   Make the independent architecture understandable in three seconds.

### Image placeholders

`[REAL PHOTO A: robot operating alone in a clear area]`\
Suggested source: `robot-alone.jpg` or `PXL_20261115_183304509.jpg`.

`[REAL PHOTO B: children interacting with or petting the robot]`\
Suggested source: `kids-petting.jpg` or `kids-interacting.jpg`.

`[SCREENSHOT: Clawmetry dashboard showing the safety alert]`\
Required file: `[clawmetry-dashboard.png]`

`[OPTIONAL PHOTO: Jetson computer mounted on the robot]`\
Required file: `[jetson-mounted.jpg]`

### Speaker script

"A robot in a clear area can operate with its available capabilities.
Around people, its behaviour must be more restricted. During the
hackathon, we added an independent Jetson computer running at the edge
beside the robot's main stack. It monitors robot state and intent,
predicts potentially unsafe next states, flags anomalies, and alerts the
fleet operator. This is our safety monitoring and assurance layer."

### Critical accuracy rules

-   Say **monitor, predict, flag, alert**.
-   Do not say the layer prevents accidents, guarantees safety, blocks
    cyberattacks, or is fail safe.
-   Do not call it certified or compliant.
-   Do not imply the layer physically stopped or locked the robot unless
    that intervention is implemented and demonstrated.
-   The team described a restricted control state when children are
    nearby. Confirm whether the layer actually enforces a control
    restriction or whether the dashboard only detects and alerts. Use
    the precise description supported by the demo.
-   If the dashboard alert is from a controlled test, label it
    **CONTROLLED TEST**. Never present a staged alert as a real
    incident.
-   Add a measured result only when supplied from logs or a repeatable
    test: `[DETECTION LATENCY: ___ ms under ___ test conditions]`.
-   Confirm whether the Jetson layer operates fully independently and
    what happens if it loses power or communication. Do not call it fail
    safe without proof.

prepaid or metered usage component. This is a recommendation to test,
not a validated price.

# 8. Q&A preparation

The pitch is followed by 2 minutes of questions. Prepare concise,
evidence based answers.

## "Is this just a hackathon prototype?"

"We have a working prototype and deployed it in a real guest
environment. During the hackathon we integrated the edge computer,
navigation, voice interaction, gestures, and escort behaviour. We are
now looking for a paid pilot with clear success measures."

Be ready to show the demo and a measured result. Do not imply production
readiness.

## "Is the robot safe around children?"

"We are developing an independent edge based monitoring and assurance
layer. It monitors robot state and intent, predicts potentially unsafe
next states, flags anomalies, and alerts an operator. We are not
claiming certification or guaranteed safety. We need further testing and
the appropriate assessment before broader deployment."

If the restriction behaviour is demonstrated, explain exactly what it
does. If it only alerts, say that.

## "What happens if the Jetson fails?"

Answer only from the implemented design. Prepare the actual behaviour
for power loss, communication loss, process crash, and restart. Do not
claim fail safe behaviour without a tested mechanism.

## "Why would a spa pay for this?"

"The deployment showed guest interest, and staff suggested a second use
case: welcoming visitors outside and inviting them in. The paid pilot is
how we will test operational value, service quality, and willingness to
pay."

Do not claim staff have committed to purchase.

## "What does it cost?"

"We are structuring it as a paid pilot, monthly service subscription, or
prepaid operating hours. We are validating the price with pilot
customers and will price against deployment and operating costs."

Insert actual prices only after the team agrees them.

## "What about privacy?"

Explain exactly where audio is processed, whether recordings are stored,
what information is retained, and how guests are informed. Do not say
"edge only" or "nothing is stored" unless verified.

## "Does the safety layer work with other robot brands?"

Only say yes if it has been tested on other platforms. Otherwise say the
architecture is designed as a separate layer, and cross platform
compatibility remains to be validated.

## "What is the next market?"

"Tourism and hospitality venues where visitors need information and
directions while staff are occupied: thermal spas, hotels, visitor
centres, and tourist information points." Treat these as target
segments, not confirmed customers.
